# Decisions and standing advice

Two kinds of thing live here: choices we have made and are not relitigating, and advice
from people who have run these events that we are choosing to operate on.

---

## Decided

### The MOLTEN theme is spatial creation, not slime
A slime / non-Newtonian prompt was the leading LA idea through mid-August. It is no
longer a symmetric choice: the spatial description, five tracks, and hour-by-hour
schedule were submitted to Tech Week and **approved on 2026-08-21**. Changing the theme
now means re-submitting an approved event and re-opening the grant. Surface that cost
before reviving it.

### Both events are one relationship, two answers
Sponsors, venues, and judges get a single entry in `people.md` with a separate line per
event. A sponsor who says yes to SF has not said yes to LA.

### Partiful and the one-pagers are canonical, not the application
`events/<city>/brief.md` records what a16z approved. It is history, not a spec, and
details are allowed to drift from it — timing especially. The binding grant condition is
the audience, not the agenda.

Reversed 2026-08-26. The earlier version of this decision said the approved text wins and
that disagreeing one-pagers were wrong. That was overcautious. One thing still needs
fixing though, and it is not a drift problem: the **MOLTEN venue one-pager asks for less
time than the event runs**, so a venue could agree to it and still not cover demos.

### Outreach does not chase the NEURAL track — PARTIALLY REVERSED 2026-08-31
Decided 2026-08-26. NEURAL stays a track at INTERFACE — it is in the approved
application and on Partiful, and students can build in it on their own hardware. But we
spend no outreach effort hunting a Neural speaker or sponsor. The research bore this out:
Echo Neurotechnologies is the only serious a16z BCI company, so the pipeline is one name
deep, and it is also the track with the largest unfunded hardware claim at ~15 EEG
headsets. Effort goes to VOICE, TOUCH, VISION, and OPEN, where the field is deep and the
companies have DevRel teams and credit programs.

This does not resolve the EEG headsets. If no hardware lands, NEURAL runs software-only
on whatever hackers bring.

**Reversed for hardware on 2026-08-31, Iana's call: neural is in scope for both events.**
What survives from the original decision: still no hunt for a NEURAL *sponsor or speaker*
(the a16z pipeline is still one name deep). What changes: we chase **hardware-loaner
asks** — Emotiv and OpenBCI already exist as issues on the Linear board — because that is
the ask that fixes the ~15-headset gap in the one-pager. Per event:
- **INTERFACE**: NEURAL is an approved track; loaner headsets slot straight in.
- **MOLTEN**: **no track change** — the five spatial tracks are in the approved Tech Week
  application and adding one means re-submitting an approved event. EEG enters as an
  input modality inside existing tracks, which the ≥2-body-input-modalities rule already
  rewards.
- The events are a week apart, so one loaner ask covers both: SF Oct 10–11, LA Oct 16–17.

## Standing advice from Alexa Orent (AGI House), 2026-08-26

She runs hackathon programming out of the AGI House SF space. This is the most
experienced operator we have talked to. Her advice, in her priority order:

### 1. Lock a speaker before scaling sponsor outreach
A known speaker is credibility, and credibility is what makes sponsor conversations
easy. Start with the personal network, then work outward to less personal asks.

### 2. Use the a16z relationship for the speaker
Ask the Tech Week grant team directly for a portfolio company speaker slot. We are
already grant recipients and Fil is already waiting on a reply from us — that reply is
the natural place to ask.

She also flagged a specific portco: **a BCI company in Austin**, a16z portfolio, doing a
lot of recruiting right now and, in her words, "just a good team." She did not name it
and offered to reach out to the founder herself. **Go through Alexa, not cold.**

That company appears to be **Echo Neurotechnologies**, which is in San Francisco rather
than Austin — see `outreach-targets.md` for how the two got conflated. Lower priority now
that we are not chasing NEURAL, but the offer is free and Alexa made it unprompted, so
it costs nothing to take.

### 3. Announce as early as possible
Tech Week is a competitive week and attention is the scarce thing. Get on people's
agendas before the other events do. This lines up with the Sept 8 calendar launch —
that date is the floor, not the target.

### 4. Venue and Wi-Fi are the things that actually break events
Past events have been derailed by connectivity. Lock the venue before scaling sponsor
outreach, and interrogate the Wi-Fi specifically rather than trusting a yes.

### 5. Skip mass tier sheets early
Do not lead with a gold/silver/bronze sponsor deck. Start with conversations and shape
the ask to the company.

### 6. Check the competitive landscape on the date
Her note on SF: look at what else is running Friday versus Sunday, because Sunday may be
less contested. Our dates are fixed, but this argues for knowing what we are up against
— LA Hacks already collides with MOLTEN.

### 7. Demo format: rooms, not a parade
Split builders into rooms and have judges rotate YC-style, so every team gets direct
feedback instead of watching a big-screen procession. Use judge deliberation time for a
community vote to keep non-finalists engaged. **Always demo** — she named a past event
where a near-winning project never got shown as a real regret.

Our approved schedules already have a rotating demo expo at 11:30 AM and a top-10 finals
at 1:30 PM, which is compatible with this.

### 8. On the day
> "Spend a lot of time thinking through every single detail, and then triple check that
> every detail is getting taken care of. But also have the understanding that something
> is going to go wrong. And it's okay."

> "I talk to every single person who comes to the door. I make that a big point."
