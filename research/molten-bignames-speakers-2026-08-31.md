## 3. Reputational Risk Flags & Mitigation Strategies

When sourcing speakers for a collegiate audience (USC, UCLA, Caltech, ArtCenter), the cultural, ethical, and political sensibilities of Gen Z students must be paramount. Several of the highest-profile candidates operate in sectors currently facing intense scrutiny.

### **Palmer Luckey (Anduril Industries)**
*   **The Risk:** Luckey’s company, Anduril, develops AI-powered autonomous weapons and surveillance systems utilized by the military and border patrol [cite: 107]. In 2025/2026, Luckey has faced intense protests at university campuses—including Cornell, Ohio State, and National Taiwan University—over his self-proclaimed status as a "radical Zionist," Anduril's alleged ties to conflicts in Gaza, and the ethics of AI weaponry [cite: 107, 108]. 
*   **Mitigation/Recommendation:** **HIGH RISK.** While his legacy as the creator of the Oculus Rift [cite: 106] is directly relevant to XR, his current persona is highly polarizing. Inviting him to a student hackathon is almost guaranteed to invite protests or boycotts from student activist groups. If invited, he must be strictly constrained to a fireside chat specifically regarding his early days building VR at USC's MxR lab [cite: 106]. However, exclusion is strongly recommended to avoid derailing the event's positive focus.

### **Edward Saatchi (Fable Studio)**
*   **The Risk:** Saatchi’s platform, *Showrunner*, has positioned itself as the "Netflix of AI," allowing users to generate their own TV episodes using AI [cite: 81, 84]. In the wake of the recent Hollywood writer and actor strikes, generative AI that replaces human writers and animators is deeply unpopular among creative students (particularly those from ArtCenter and USC's film/games programs). Furthermore, his unauthorized AI generation of *South Park* episodes [cite: 81, 83] raises massive IP and ethical red flags.
*   **Mitigation/Recommendation:** **MODERATE TO HIGH RISK.** Students may view his platform as a threat to their future careers in the entertainment industry [cite: 83]. If he speaks, the moderation must be rigorous. The interviewer should press him on creator compensation models (he claims creators will get a 40% cut if others build on their IP [cite: 84]) and the ethics of synthetic media. Frame his session as a debate on "The Ethics of Generative Worldbuilding" rather than an endorsement.

### **Chris Ume (Metaphysic)**
*   **The Risk:** Ume is a pioneer of hyper-realistic deepfakes (e.g., DeepTomCruise) [cite: 79]. While the technology is astounding, deepfakes are intrinsically linked to misinformation, non-consensual imagery, and identity theft. 
*   **Mitigation/Recommendation:** **LOW TO MODERATE RISK.** Ume is highly aware of these issues and actively advocates for ethical AI use and biometric data rights [cite: 78]. He is generally well-received as an artist. Ensure his talk focuses heavily on consent, ethical web3 economies, and how Metaphysic works *with* actors (like Tom Hanks in the film *Here*) [cite: 77] rather than replacing them.

### **Tye Sheridan (Wonder Dynamics / Autodesk)**
*   **The Risk:** Wonder Dynamics automates VFX and CGI processes [cite: 58]. Similar to Saatchi, there is a lingering fear among VFX/animation students that this technology aims to eliminate entry-level industry jobs.
*   **Mitigation/Recommendation:** **LOW RISK.** Sheridan navigates this well by leaning on his background as an artist and actor. He consistently frames Wonder Dynamics as a tool for *democratization*, allowing indie filmmakers with no budget to create blockbuster-level sci-fi [cite: 57, 113]. Ensure his messaging remains strictly on "empowering the indie creator" rather than "cutting studio costs."
