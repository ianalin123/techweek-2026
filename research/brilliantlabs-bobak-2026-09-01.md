## 2. Commercial Posture and Halo Status

Brilliant Labs' commercial strategy has evolved from niche developer tools to consumer-facing AI wearables. However, as of late 2026, their operational reality is marked by severe supply chain distress.

### 2.1 The Evolution of the Hardware Ecosystem
The company’s product lineage demonstrates a clear trajectory toward everyday wearability:
1.  **Monocle (2023):** An open-source, clip-on AR device designed strictly as a developer kit. It featured a hackable FPGA and was embraced by hackers [cite: 11, 18].
2.  **Frame (2024):** A pair of lightweight (39g) smart glasses resembling John Lennon's spectacles. They featured a microOLED display, a multimodal AI assistant named Noa, and were heavily praised for their open-source SDK (Python/Lua) [cite: 2, 19]. **Status as of late 2026:** Discontinued [cite: 5, 6].
3.  **Halo (Announced 2025):** Positioned as the world's thinnest AI glasses, featuring a color microOLED display, dual bone-conduction speakers, an Alif B1 AI processor (with NPU), a 14-hour battery, and a $299–$349 price point [cite: 20, 21]. 

### 2.2 The "Halo" Platform and Software Capabilities
Halo represents a massive leap in onboard edge AI. Partnering with Neuphonic (for ultra-low-latency voice AI) and TheStage AI (for on-device model optimization), Halo is designed to process visual and audio data locally, without sending raw data to the cloud [cite: 22, 23]. 
Key software features include:
*   **Noa:** The conversational AI agent [cite: 24, 25].
*   **Narrative:** A long-term, privacy-first memory system that indexes the user's daily life for later recall [cite: 24, 26].
*   **Vibe Mode:** An experimental natural-language interface allowing users to generate custom mini-apps simply by speaking (e.g., "build me a custom navigation app") [cite: 25, 26].

### 2.3 Unit Availability and The Manufacturing Crisis (Late 2026)
While the technological promises of Halo are staggering, the commercial reality is dire. Halo was opened for pre-orders in July 2025 with an expected shipping date of November 2025 [cite: 27, 28]. However, the company missed this target and fell into a cascading series of delays. 

According to robust community feedback on Reddit throughout 2026, backers have been subjected to a "never-ending saga" of excuses [cite: 3, 29]. Promised delivery windows shifted from December 2025, to Q1 2026, to late spring, and into the summer [cite: 3]. Customers report that Brilliant Labs has failed to send review units to tech influencers, citing that they are "too busy to handle that" [cite: 3]. Furthermore, the company has reportedly muted or banned users on their Discord who complain about the delays, and customers who ordered prescription lenses are trapped by high cancellation fees [cite: 3, 4]. 

### 2.4 Hiring and Organizational Bandwidth
The root cause of these delays appears to be a severe lack of organizational bandwidth. Brilliant Labs was founded by a core trio: Bobak Tavangar (CEO), Raj Nakarja (Head Engineer), and Ben Heald (Head Designer) [cite: 13, 30]. While Caplight estimates their employee range at around 10 people [cite: 1] (and highly speculative aggregator databases list 51-200 [cite: 31]), direct communications with frustrated backers reveal that Brilliant Labs still operates effectively as a "3-person company" at its core [cite: 3]. This ultra-lean structure explains the bottleneck in scaling mass production with their Singaporean manufacturing partner, Moveon Technologies [cite: 30].
