## The Five Tracks of Spatial Creation: Theoretical Framework

To map the candidates effectively, it is necessary to establish a rigorous theoretical framework for the five tracks of the MOLTEN hackathon. These tracks represent the core ontological pillars of spatial computing.

### 1. WORLDBUILD (3D Environments and Rendering)
Worldbuilding encompasses the architectural and environmental construction of virtual spaces. From an engineering perspective, this involves the optimization of rendering pipelines and physically based shading. The rendering equation, fundamental to this track, is expressed as:
\[ L_o(\mathbf{x}, \omega_o, \lambda, t) = L_e(\mathbf{x}, \omega_o, \lambda, t) + \int_{\Omega} f_r(\mathbf{x}, \omega_i, \omega_o, \lambda, t) L_i(\mathbf{x}, \omega_i, \lambda, t) (\omega_i \cdot \mathbf{n}) \operatorname{d}\omega_i \]
Candidates mapped to this track specialize in game engines (Unreal, proprietary console engines) and the artistic direction required to make these spaces emotionally resonant and performant.

### 2. NAVIGATE (Spatial UIs and Locative Media)
Navigation refers to how humans interface with spatial computers. It transcends 2D planar screens, requiring an understanding of depth, occlusion, and gestural inputs. This track focuses on AR glasses (such as Snap Spectacles), mixed reality headsets (Apple Vision Pro), and the user experience (UX) paradigms that guide users through hybrid physical-digital environments. 

### 3. CAPTURE (Reality Capture and Gaussian Splatting)
The Capture track is defined by the digitization of the physical world. While traditional photogrammetry relied on polygonal meshes, 2026 workflows heavily integrate 3D Gaussian Splatting, which represents scenes as a collection of 3D Gaussians characterized by a covariance matrix \(\Sigma\) and mean \(\mu\):
\[ G(\mathbf{x}) = e^{-\frac{1}{2} (\mathbf{x} - \mu)^T \Sigma^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \mu)} \]
Experts in this track work in virtual production volumes (e.g., Amazon MGM Stage 15), ray-tracing algorithm development (NVIDIA), and advanced machine perception.

### 4. INHABIT (Avatars, Rigging, and Identity)
To inhabit a spatial environment is to possess a digital body. This track explores character rigging, technical animation, and the translation of human kinematics into virtual spaces. Forward kinematics for an avatar's joint chain can be represented by transformation matrices:
\[ T_{0}^{n} = \prod_{i=1}^{n} T_{i-1}^{i}(\theta_i) \]
Technical artists from studios like Riot Games and Blizzard excel in this domain, creating systems that allow users and characters to express complex emotions and actions.

### 5. OPEN (Interoperability and Ecosystems)
The Open track focuses on the foundational infrastructure that allows spatial assets to traverse different platforms seamlessly. This includes advocating for Universal Scene Description (OpenUSD), glTF, and open-source software integration, ensuring that spatial creation is not siloed within proprietary walled gardens.

### 1.1 The Theoretical Underpinnings of Spatial Creation
Spatial creation relies on complex mathematical and computational frameworks. For instance, the rendering of photorealistic virtual environments in real-time LED volumes relies heavily on the rendering equation:
\[ L_o(x, \omega_o) = L_e(x, \omega_o) + \int_{\Omega} f_r(x, \omega_i, \omega_o) L_i(x, \omega_i) (\omega_i \cdot n) d\omega_i \]
Furthermore, reality capture and scene reconstruction have been revolutionized by 3D Gaussian Splatting, which represents scenes as continuous volumetric radiance fields parameterized by 3D Gaussians. The covariance matrix \(\Sigma\) of these splats, defined by scaling \(S\) and rotation \(R\) matrices, is expressed as:
\[ \Sigma = R S S^T R^T \]
Understanding these mathematical foundations is critical for the Caltech and USC computer science contingents. Therefore, the selected speakers possess not only creative vision but the technical acumen to discuss these pipelines.
