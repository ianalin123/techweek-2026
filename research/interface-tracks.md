# Strategic Analysis of Target Entities for the INTERFACE 2026 Hackathon

**Key Points:**
*   **Strong alignment exists between a16z portfolio companies and student hackathons.** Research suggests that venture-backed firms in the human-computer interaction space frequently utilize hackathons for developer relations (DevRel) and talent acquisition.
*   **Physical AI and teleoperation represent highly emergent fields.** It seems likely that targets focused on robotics teleoperation and multimodal integration will resonate strongly with UC Berkeley and Stanford engineering cohorts.
*   **The landscape of AI coding and agentic workflows has shifted dramatically by 2026.** The evidence leans toward an industry-wide pivot toward "vibe coding" and multi-agent systems, necessitating hackathon sponsors that provide robust API infrastructure.

**Contextualizing the 2026 HCI Landscape**
The transition from traditional graphical user interfaces (GUIs) to multimodal, agentic, and physically embodied systems marks a critical juncture in computer science. As of late 2025 and 2026, foundation models have matured to process text, audio, and visual data natively, bypassing the latency and compression issues of "stitched" architectures [cite: 1]. This hackathon, focused on **VOICE**, **TOUCH**, **VISION**, and **OPEN** interfaces, operates at the nexus of these advancements. 

**Methodological Approach**
This report synthesizes extensive industry data to identify the most viable sponsors, speakers, and judges for the INTERFACE 2026 student hackathon in San Francisco. The methodology deliberately excludes brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) and neurotechnology to adhere strictly to the established scope. Evaluation criteria prioritize local Bay Area presence (facilitating Saturday attendance), existing partnerships with Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), demonstrated financial and operational commitment to student hackathons, and aggressive hiring postures indicative of a desire to recruit top-tier talent from UC Berkeley and Stanford.

### Detailed Analysis of VOICE Targets

**ElevenLabs** has established itself as a dominant force in AI audio generation. Their technology spans text-to-speech, voice cloning, and conversational AI [cite: 2, 3]. For the INTERFACE hackathon, ElevenLabs represents an ideal "marquee" sponsor. They have a direct and highly publicized relationship with a16z, which co-led their $180 million Series C [cite: 2]. Their infrastructure is robust, and they actively issue API credits to hackathon participants, making them a frictionless integration for student developers [cite: 35, 36].

**Cartesia** operates directly within the Bay Area ecosystem, having been founded by researchers from the Stanford AI Lab [cite: 14]. They build State Space Models (SSMs) for real-time generative audio, achieving state-of-the-art results [cite: 14]. Cartesia is deeply embedded in the local hacker culture; in late 2024, they brought together over 2,000 builders in San Francisco for a hackathon, giving away $20,000 in prizes [cite: 16]. They also co-hosted a voice agents hackathon with Anthropic in SF [cite: 15]. Because their founding team consists of Stanford alumni, they possess a strong cultural alignment with the Stanford demographic of the INTERFACE audience. While their primary leads are Kleiner Perkins and Index Ventures, they are deeply involved with AGI House (which has ties to a16z) [cite: 16, 30].

**SLNG.ai** serves as an orchestration and infrastructure layer for real-time speech, aggregating models from providers like Deepgram and Cartesia to ensure ultra-low latency (under 200ms) [cite: 18]. What makes SLNG.ai a prime target is its hyper-active Developer Relations strategy. Nicolas Grenié, their Lead DevRel, has a long history of organizing and attending hackathons, providing on-site mentorship, and building custom SDKs and n8n nodes specifically to help hackers move faster during 24-hour events [cite: 17, 18]. 

**Deepgram** provides highly accurate, real-time Speech-to-Text (STT) APIs [cite: 33]. They have a well-documented history of supporting developers through API credits, routinely offering $150 free credit tiers for builders [cite: 32, 37]. They have historically partnered with platforms like DEV to run asynchronous hackathons [cite: 33]. Their established developer support infrastructure makes them a low-friction sponsor for technical integrations.

**Valence AI (Valence Vibrations)** bridges the gap between VOICE and TOUCH. Founded by Chloe Duckworth and Shannon Brownlee while they were students at USC, the company originated from a hackathon project [cite: 25, 34]. Valence builds emotion AI that analyzes vocal tones and prosody in real-time, often translating this data into haptic feedback (e.g., via Apple Watch) to aid neurodivergent individuals in reading emotional cues [cite: 27, 28]. Having recently raised a $5 million seed round, they are actively hiring [cite: 34]. Duckworth is an incredibly compelling speaker who advocates for empathetic AI, making her an ideal judge or keynote speaker [cite: 26, 28].

### Detailed Analysis of TOUCH Targets

**XDOF** represents a critical evolution in physical AI infrastructure. Founded in 2024 by UC Berkeley PhD researchers Philipp Wu, Fred Shentu, and Nemo Jin, XDOF solves the massive data bottleneck in robotics by building teleoperation and annotation pipelines [cite: 7, 9]. They recently emerged from stealth with $70 million in funding, backed heavily by a16z [cite: 8, 38]. XDOF's relevance to the INTERFACE hackathon is twofold. First, their work directly maps to the TOUCH track; they utilize teleoperation systems (like GELLO) and egocentric wearable sensors to capture human motor intent and translate it into robot foundation models [cite: 9]. **Skydio**, a leading autonomous drone manufacturer based in San Mateo, relies heavily on complex spatial computing, computer vision, and physical hardware interfaces [cite: 40]. They have a history of sponsoring drone hackathons and STEM competitions (e.g., UAS4STEM), providing judges and mentors [cite: 42]. For students interested in the physical deployment of AI and hardware control loops, Skydio is a highly relevant local sponsor.

**Ultraleap** is a foundational company in the field of mid-air haptics and hand-tracking [cite: 43]. Their hardware allows users to feel tactile feedback in mid-air using ultrasound, entirely replacing traditional physical controllers. While their corporate HQ and a16z status are unverified, their technology is a staple at XR and VR hackathons (such as the MIT Reality Hack) [cite: 44, 45]. Securing Ultraleap hardware for the hackathon would drastically elevate the quality of projects in the TOUCH and VR/AR domains.

## 6. Proven Hackathon Programs, API Credits, and Developer Grants

Securing technical sponsorships requires leveraging established programs. The following companies possess formal mechanisms for supporting student builders.

*   **Vercel for Startups**: Vercel offers a formalized program granting up to $30,000 in flexible commitment credits to startups affiliated with approved partners (explicitly including a16z). *   **ElevenLabs Hackathon Support**: ElevenLabs routinely sponsors external hackathons and offers API credits. They have a history of running their own asynchronous hackathons (e.g., on the DEV community platform) where they distribute significant cash and swag prizes [cite: 32, 33].
*   **Cursor Ambassador Program**: Cursor operates a community program that explicitly provides funding to cover costs for community meetups and hackathons. Ambassadors also receive free Cursor credits and direct communication channels with the Cursor team [cite: 49]. 
*   **SLNG.ai Hackathon Integrations**: SLNG.ai actively builds custom integrations for hackathons. For instance, they shipped a custom n8n community node specifically for the {Tech: Europe} London AI Hackathon to help students drop voice agents directly into visual workflows [cite: 18]. 
*   **Roboflow Educational Support**: Roboflow heavily supports academic and student projects, offering robust open-source tools and frequent mentorship at major collegiate hackathons like MHacks [cite: 23, 47].
